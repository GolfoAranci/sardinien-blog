Golfo Aranci: More Than a Ferry Port

Most people only know Golfo Aranci from the ferry quay: arrive, start the car, drive on. Yet at the far northeastern tip of Sardinia lies a town with pale sandy coves, a protected headland on the doorstep and restaurants worth a visit. This guide gathers what you should know for a stay and points to our detailed articles.

Getting there: ferry, airport, train

By ferry: According to ferry portals, the ships from Golfo Aranci are mainly run by Corsica Ferries. The connection to Livorno (7 to 10 hours) runs all year, with two departures a week in winter and daily from April. In summer Genoa (about 10 to 11 hours) is added, in August Piombino, and seasonally Bastia on Corsica. Timetables change, so check with the shipping line. How day and night ferries differ and which port is worth it is covered in our articles Day Ferry or Night Ferry? and Ferry Ports Compared.

By air: Olbia Costa Smeralda airport is about 16 kilometres away, roughly 20 minutes by car. According to timetable portals, train and bus connect Olbia and Golfo Aranci in about 20 to 25 minutes; the station is around 500 metres from the port. More in the airport comparison and, for the car, in our article Booking a Rental Car.

The beaches of Golfo Aranci

Golfo Aranci is known for pale, fine sandy beaches with gently sloping water. The town's tourist information describes numerous beaches and coves. A selection:

  • Spiaggia Bianca – pale, fine, soft sand and clear, azure water. There is a kiosk right at the beach; the access is signposted from the provincial road towards Olbia, past the roundabout.
  • Cala Sassari – a pale, fine sandy cove with clear, green water and a shallow, sandy bottom.
  • Baia Caddinas – golden, fine to medium sand and green water, with parking, rental and a restaurant. Da Andrea, number 2 on our restaurant ranking, is here too.
  • Cala Moresca – small coves with golden, fine sand at the foot of Capo Figari. No facilities, an old lighthouse nearby; part of the way can be driven, the rest is on foot.
  • Cala del Sonno – pebbles instead of sand and a rocky bottom; reachable only by boat or via a demanding hiking trail along Capo Figari.

Further beaches such as Sos Aranzos, Nodu Pianu and Cala Banana lie towards Olbia and Pittulongu. An overview of the most beautiful beaches across the whole island, with a map, is in our beaches article.

If you stay in Golfo Aranci you don't have to drive far: the beaches are between a few minutes and a quarter of an hour from the town centre.

Capo Figari and the island of Figarolo

The headland juts into the sea at Sardinia's northeastern tip. It is protected as a site of community importance and described by Sardegna Turismo as an "open-air natural museum". The summit reaches about 350 metres. From Cala Moresca it is a good three kilometres to the top, all uphill; for the hike via Cala Moresca allow roughly three to three and a half hours, depending on the route, with steep and rocky sections.

The reward is the view: to the north lie the islands of Soffi and Mortorio off the Costa Smeralda, to the south the Gulf of Olbia with Capo Ceraso and Tavolara. In front of Cala Moresca lies the pyramid-shaped island of Figarolo. According to Sardegna Turismo, mouflon, cormorants and buzzards live there, alongside holm oaks, mastic shrubs and wild olive trees; the surrounding waters are rich in groupers and spiny lobsters.

Practical tip: For Capo Figari, pack sturdy shoes, water and sun protection, and in high summer start early. Trips to the neighbouring island of Tavolara are in our excursions article.

A glance at local history

According to the municipality, the name Golfo Aranci dates back to 1839 and is attributed to General Alberto Ferrero della Marmora; there are several interpretations of its origin. Because the bay at Terranova, today's Olbia, was silting up, the scheduled service to Civitavecchia was moved in 1880 to Figari, as the place was then called. The railway line from Terranova to Golfo Aranci opened on 1 July 1883, and from 1961 train ferries ran to the mainland from here. So the town has been a port and a station from the beginning, and that still shapes it today.

Food and festivals

Where to eat well in Golfo Aranci is in our personal ranking of the ten best restaurants. In August the town holds the Sagra del Pesce on Piazza Cossiga: volunteers from the Pro Loco fry squid, prawns and sole in huge pans. In 2026 it was the 58th edition, on 14 August. The date changes, so check each year.

Please note: Timetables, opening hours and events change. Before your trip, check with the shipping line, transport operators and the municipality of Golfo Aranci.
