Practical Travel Tips for Sardinia

A few organizational points that are often underestimated when preparing a Sardinia trip – from power outlets to the current trend around beach reservations.

Best time to visit

July and August are peak season: packed beaches, high prices, sometimes access restrictions at popular coves. June, September, and early October usually still offer warm swimming weather with noticeably less hustle – a good compromise for anyone not tied to school holidays.

Before your trip

  • Pack a travel adapter: Italy uses plug types F/L. Travelers from Switzerland (type J) or the UK (type G) need an adapter.
  • Check your travel health insurance: especially when traveling by ferry or flight, it's worth reviewing your existing policy.
  • Book a rental car early: without your own car or ferry travel, a rental car is practically essential in Sardinia – public transport outside the larger cities is sparse.
  • Keep an eye on beach reservations: an increasing number of the island's best-known coves (including in the Golfo di Orosei and around San Teodoro) now require an online reservation with a visitor cap from June to September – a growing trend to protect coastal ecosystems. Research the relevant portals and deadlines (often just a few days ahead) in advance.

Packing

  • Sturdy shoes for hikes or boat excursions.
  • Sunscreen with a high SPF – the sun in Sardinia is strong even outside peak season.
  • A light sweater or thin jacket for cooler evenings, especially in spring and autumn.
  • A snorkel set, if you have one – many coves are excellent for it.
A current trend across the island: more and more nature reserves and popular beaches use visitor caps and online reservations instead of free access – advance planning matters more than it used to.

On the ground

  • Plan drive times realistically: Sardinia looks compact on a map, but winding coastal roads and drives through towns cost time. Budget generously for trips between regions.
  • Cash for smaller places: card payment isn't accepted everywhere, especially at small beach bars or markets.
  • Restaurant reservations a day ahead: even outside peak season, popular addresses fill up quickly on nice evenings.
In short: a plug adapter, a booked rental car, and timely beach reservations take out most of the organizational stress in advance – the rest can stay spontaneous.
