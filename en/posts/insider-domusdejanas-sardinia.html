Domus de Janas: Sardinia's Fairy Houses

Long before the first nuraghi were built, the people of Sardinia carved elaborate burial chambers into the rock – around 3,500 of them survive to this day.

What Domus de Janas are

The name literally means "houses of the fairies." They are Neolithic tomb chambers carved directly into soft rock between roughly 4400 and 2300 BC – by the Ozieri culture and other pre-nuraghic cultures on the island.

Recreating a house for the dead

Inside, gabled roofs, columns, hearths, and even stone beds were recreated – a deliberate attempt to give the dead a home within the rock. Some walls still show traces of red ochre and bull's-head symbols, presumably intended as protective symbols.

Good to know: in July 2025, 17 selected Domus de Janas sites were officially declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site – a timely reason to look more closely at this lesser-known precursor to the nuraghic culture.
Almost 2,000 years before the first nuraghi, these rock tombs already shaped the Sardinian landscape.

Why they're barely known

Overshadowed by the better-known nuraghi and the Giants of Mont'e Prama, the Domus de Janas are forgotten by most Sardinia travelers – even though they're among the oldest surviving traces of human settlement on the island.
