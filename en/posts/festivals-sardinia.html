Traditional Festivals of Sardinia: Sant'Efisio, Sartiglia & Mamuthones

If you can be flexible with your travel dates, take a look at Sardinia's festival calendar: some of Italy's most striking traditions happen on only a few days each year.

Sant'Efisio in Cagliari (May 1–4)

Sardinia's biggest religious procession dates back to a vow made by the city of Cagliari in 1652, after a plague epidemic had killed nearly half the population. Ever since, the statue of Saint Efisio is carried each year in a festive procession roughly 65 kilometres from Cagliari to Nora and back – accompanied by tractors, ox carts, and thousands of participants in traditional costumes from every region of the island. It's considered one of Italy's longest and oldest religious processions.

Sa Sartiglia in Oristano (Carnival)

Since 1543, Oristano's old town transforms into a jousting arena on Carnival Sunday and Tuesday: masked riders gallop at full speed through the narrow streets, trying to spear a star hanging from a ribbon with a sword. The spectacle is led by the "Su Componidori," whose elaborate dressing ceremony is itself a small ritual.

According to superstition, the more rings the riders hit at the Sartiglia, the richer the coming harvest will be.

Mamuthones e Issohadores in Mamoiada (Carnival)

One of the island's oldest and most enigmatic festivals: wearing black wooden masks, heavy sheepskins, and clanking cowbells, the "Mamuthones" move through Mamoiada in the Barbagia in a heavy, deliberate rhythm, accompanied by the red-clad "Issohadores." The ritual's exact origin is still debated today – pre-Christian roots are suspected.

Cavalcata Sarda in Sassari (second-to-last Sunday in May)

Originally created in 1711 as a tribute to the Spanish king, the Cavalcata Sarda is today a grand procession of traditional costumes from virtually every region of Sardinia, followed by horse races in the afternoon.

Practical tip: accommodation in the relevant towns (especially Cagliari for Sant'Efisio) books out early for the big festivals – if you want to attend, book months in advance.
