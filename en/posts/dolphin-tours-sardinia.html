Dolphin Tours in Sardinia: Where and What to Look For

The waters around Sardinia are among the most dolphin-rich in the western Mediterranean. Where your odds of a sighting are best – and what to look for when choosing an operator.

Where dolphins are spotted most often

  • Golfo Aranci & surroundings (Northeast): considered one of the most reliable areas of the island for dolphin sightings, including around the offshore Isola Figarolo.
  • La Maddalena Archipelago (North): dolphins are regularly around here, often spotted during a standard island tour.
  • Golfo di Orosei (East): occasional sightings are possible on the way to remote coves like Cala Luna or Cala Goloritzé, though less reliably than in the northeast.

What makes a good tour

  • Small boats instead of mass excursions: smaller groups disturb the animals less and also improve your own chances of a good view.
  • Responsible behaviour: reputable operators approach the animals slowly, don't actively "chase" the dolphins, and give them room to move away.
  • No guarantee: dolphins are wild animals – reputable operators offer a chance of a sighting, never a guarantee.
A good sign of a responsible operator: they explain upfront how the crew approaches the animals – and what happens if the dolphins show no interest.

Combining with other activities

Many dolphin boat tours are combined with snorkeling stops at nearby coves or a loop around striking islands (e.g. Tavolara or Figarolo) – a good reason to still have a satisfying day even without a dolphin sighting.

Practical tip: early morning tours are often considered a bit calmer and more promising than afternoon ones, when there's more boat traffic. If you're prone to seasickness, also factor in swell and weather conditions.
