Cala Domestica: The Bay of the Old Mines

A deep bay with golden sand, surrounded by high cliffs, an old watchtower on the headland and traces of mining in the sand: Cala Domestica lies on the southwest coast between Buggerru and Iglesias.

Location & access

Cala Domestica lies two kilometres from Buggerru on the southwest coast, on the border with the area of Iglesias. There is a large car park, a motorhome stopover, a snack bar and a campsite nearby (more in our article Sardinia by Motorhome). From there a wooden walkway leads to the beach in about seven to eight minutes. According to guides, parking costs around 5 euros a day – prices change, so check on site.

Beach & bay

The beach is about a hundred metres wide with golden sand, and the bay is framed by high cliffs covered in macchia. On the headland stands a watchtower from the Spanish period, with wide views along the coast.

Wild, steep and still easy to reach: one of the most striking bays in the southwest.

Traces of mining

Until 1940 the ore extracted in the nearby mines was shipped from here. Remains of warehouses and galleries can still be seen on the beach. To the right of the main beach, a tunnel dug by the miners leads to a second, small cove, Sa Caletta.

Practical tip: Further north, the dune landscape of Piscinas beckons – together the two make a fine day on the coast of the old mining region.
