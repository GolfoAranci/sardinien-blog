The Most Beautiful Beaches in Sardinia

Sardinia rightly counts as one of the most beach-rich islands in the Mediterranean – from Caribbean-looking coves to sand dunes stretching for kilometres. An overview of the best-known destinations, sorted by region.

Northwest: Around Stintino

Spiaggia della Pelosa is considered by many to be the island's most beautiful beach: Caribbean-style turquoise water, fine white sand, and a view of the offshore Torre della Pelosa. Popular accordingly – if you want peace and quiet, come early or in the shoulder season.

East: Golfo di Orosei

The steep coastline of the Golfo di Orosei is home to some of the island's most spectacular coves, most reachable only by boat or on foot:

  • Cala Goloritzé – a UNESCO natural monument with a striking rock arch.
  • Cala Mariolu – round white pebbles and emerald-green water.
  • Cala Luna – caves and Mediterranean scrubland, also reachable by boat or hiking trail.
  • Cala Sisine – secluded at the end of a gorge.

Northeast: San Teodoro & Costa Smeralda

  • La Cinta – around five kilometres of wide sandy beach at San Teodoro, with a flamingo lagoon behind it.
  • Cala Brandinchi – also called "little Tahiti", flat turquoise water, ideal for families.
  • Spiaggia del Principe – pale, fine quartz sand between granite rocks on the upscale Costa Smeralda.
  • S'Isuledda near Puntaldia – Caribbean-looking, fine sand.
  • Isola dei Gabbiani near Palau – not a classic swimming beach, but one of the best kite- and windsurfing spots in Europe.
Many of the best-known coves – especially in the Golfo di Orosei and around San Teodoro – now have visitor caps or reservation requirements. More on that in our tips article.

West: Sinis Peninsula

Is Arutas is a one-of-a-kind spot: the "sand" is made of tiny, rice-grain-shaped quartz pebbles instead of ordinary sand – so taking any home as a souvenir is strongly discouraged (it's now banned and checked).

Southwest

  • Chia – dune landscape, turquoise water, and a historic watchtower.
  • Tuerredda – small cove with an offshore islet, family-friendly.
  • Piscinas on the Costa Verde – spectacular shifting dunes, wild and pristine.
  • Cala Domestica – a former mining cove near Iglesias, surrounded by tall cliffs.

Southeast & South

  • Costa Rei – kilometres of fine sandy beach with gently sloping water.
  • Porto Giunco near Villasimius – between the sea and a salt lake, often with flamingos.
  • Spiaggia del Poetto near Cagliari – eight kilometres of city beach with good infrastructure, ideal if you're based in the capital.
Practical tip: Sardinia is big – driving times between regions are easy to underestimate. If you want to see several of these beaches, plan for multiple bases on the island rather than a single location (see also our article on destinations).
