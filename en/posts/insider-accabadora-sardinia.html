Sa Femina Accabadora: Legend or Reality?

One of the darkest figures in Sardinian folklore: women dressed in black said to have brought a "good death" to those suffering terminally – to this day it's disputed whether they ever really existed.

Who was the Accabadora?

The name derives from the Sardinian "s'acabbu" (the end) or the Spanish "acabar" (to finish). According to tradition, the Accabadora was a respected but also feared woman in the village community who, at the family's request, helped the terminally ill die faster and more painlessly.

Historically disputed

Whether this practice actually existed in this form is not conclusively settled among historians – concrete, contemporary evidence is scarce. A museum in Arzachena (Gallura) is dedicated to the topic and documents the traditional accounts without definitively resolving the historical facts.

Good to know: regardless of the historical truth, the Accabadora is firmly anchored in Sardinia's collective memory – she keeps appearing in novels, films, and folklore exhibitions across the island.
Between documented history and oral tradition, the line in Sardinia is often very thin.

Why the legend still fascinates today

The story touches on a topic still hotly debated today: euthanasia and how to handle incurable suffering. That a rural island society apparently developed its own cultural answer to this makes the story remarkable even from today's perspective.
