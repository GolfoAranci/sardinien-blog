Hiking in Sardinia: The Most Beautiful Routes

Sardinia is far more than beaches: the Supramonte massif in the island's east is one of the most striking and wildest hiking areas in Italy.

Selvaggio Blu – the "Wild Blue"

The Selvaggio Blu is considered Italy's most demanding long-distance trail: roughly 40 to 50 kilometres along the steep coast of the Golfo di Orosei, with rappelling sections, climbing passages, and trail-less terrain. Multi-day, recommended only with a mountain guide and proper equipment – not for a spontaneous day trip.

Tiscali – the hidden mountain village

Considerably more accessible, but no less striking: the climb to the nuragic village of Tiscali, hidden inside a doline (a collapsed cave system) in the middle of a mountain. Most routes start from Valle di Lanaitto or Su Gologone, and the round trip takes roughly three to five hours depending on the route. Find out why the village is hidden the way it is, and how old it really is, in our (German-language) dedicated article on Tiscali.

If you'd rather flip the itinerary: many operators combine hikes to Cala Luna, Cala Sisine, or Cala Goloritzé with a boat ride back – arrive on foot, return by sea.

Gola Su Gorropu – the deep gorge

One of Europe's deepest gorges, with sheer rock walls up to 400 metres high. The approach from the access road is manageable even for less experienced hikers; anyone wanting to go deeper into the gorge should check current conditions locally first.

Punta La Marmora – the highest point

At 1,834 metres, Sardinia's highest peak, in the inland Gennargentu massif. The climb is physically demanding but technically straightforward – a good choice for anyone wanting a sweeping view across the whole island.

Practical tip: the Supramonte has almost no signage and little phone coverage – for any route beyond the easy approaches, a local guide or at least an offline GPS map is strongly recommended.
