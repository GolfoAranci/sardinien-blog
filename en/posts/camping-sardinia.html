Camping in Sardinia: The Most Beautiful Regions

For anyone who wants to escape mass tourism a little and sleep closer to the beach and nature, camping in Sardinia is a popular and usually much cheaper alternative to a hotel.

Costa Rei & Southeast

Several larger campsites lie right behind the kilometres-long sandy beaches of the Costa Rei, with good infrastructure (supermarket, restaurants, sometimes pools) – such as Camping Village Capo Ferrato, a good choice for a first camping holiday on the island. There's also a very popular site at San Teodoro right on La Cinta.

Golfo di Orosei & Ogliastra

Around Cala Gonone, Dorgali, and further south in the Ogliastra, sites are often nestled between mountains and sea – especially scenic, though with a winding drive to get there (see our article on driving).

One advantage of camping away from the big hotel resorts: short distances to less crowded beaches that aren't reachable on foot from many holiday villages.

Costa Verde & Piscinas

In the southwest, around the spectacular shifting dunes of Piscinas, there are simpler, nature-close sites – wild, unspoiled, and noticeably less visited than the coasts in the northeast. More in our dedicated article on the Costa Verde.

Northeast & La Maddalena

Around Palau and opposite the Maddalena archipelago, you'll also find campsites with direct sea views – handy if a multi-day stay in the archipelago or an island excursion (see our sailing article) is on the agenda. Right by Palau also lies the Isola dei Gabbiani, where camping can be combined with one of the best kite- and windsurfing spots in Europe.

Practical notes

  • Book early: in peak season (July/August), good pitches and bungalows are often booked out months in advance.
  • Wild camping isn't allowed: free camping outside official sites is penalized in Sardinia, as across Italy.
  • Plan for shade: sites with old pine cover are noticeably more pleasant in high summer than fully open fields.
  • Motorhome pitches (Aree di sosta) exist alongside classic campsites in many coastal towns, often simpler and cheaper.
Practical tip: if you want to switch between several regions, it's best to plan no more than two or three base changes – drive times between coastal regions are longer than the map suggests.
