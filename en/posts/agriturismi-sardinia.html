Agriturismi in Sardinia: Eating Like the Locals

Some of the best meals in Sardinia aren't found in coastal restaurants, but on farms inland. An agriturismo here is, first and foremost, one thing: a place to eat – not necessarily to stay overnight.

What makes an agriturismo special as a restaurant

An agriturismo is a working farm that cooks a portion of its own produce directly into the menu: vegetables from its own garden, meat from its own livestock, cheese from its own herd, oil from its own olives. Usually there's a fixed menu with little choice, but several courses – antipasti, a pasta course, a meat course, dessert, and often a homemade digestivo to finish.

What signals a good menu

  • Antipasti plate with homemade cheese and cured meats: a strong sign of in-house production rather than bought-in goods.
  • Hand-formed culurgiones or malloreddus: recognizable by their more irregular shape compared to industrial pasta.
  • Porceddu (suckling pig) on the spit: a classic festive dish that good agriturismi also serve outside of holidays.
  • A digestivo "on the house": many agriturismi make their own Mirto or Fil'e Ferru and serve it at the end of the meal.
An agriturismo menu is rarely à la carte – usually there's one fixed menu for everyone at the table. If you have dietary restrictions, mention them when booking.

Practical notes

  • Reservations are mandatory: unlike coastal restaurants, agriturismi don't rely on walk-in trade – without booking ahead, the kitchen often stays cold.
  • Bring time: an agriturismo meal with several courses easily takes two to three hours.
  • Plan for a location away from the coast: the best addresses are often inland – a rental car is practically essential, and staying nearby can be worthwhile.
  • Well-known addresses as a starting point for your own search: places like Antichi Ovili (Orroli), Tenuta Don Ballore (Galtellì), or Agriturismo San Giovanni (near Olbia) are regularly praised for their food in travel reports – a good starting point for further research and checking current reviews.
Practical tip: Fixed-price menus usually run between 25 and 40 euros per person including drinks – good value for the number of courses and quality of ingredients.
