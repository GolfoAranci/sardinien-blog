Orgosolo: The Village of Murals

In a mountain village in the Barbagia, once known mainly for its bandits, one of Italy's most striking political mural traditions began in the late 1960s.

The origin: protest against a military base

In 1969, the residents of Orgosolo successfully resisted the conversion of their communal pastures into a military training ground at Pratobello. This protest produced the village's first political mural.

How the tradition grew

Starting in 1975, the Tuscan-born art teacher Francesco Del Casino, together with his students, began painting further walls – to mark the 30th anniversary of Italy's liberation. Since then, new works by local and international artists have continued to be added.

Good to know: today around 150 murals decorate the walls of Orgosolo's houses. Themes range from the living conditions of shepherds to the emigration of Sardinian miners to world events like apartheid – a free, freely accessible open-air walk through the village.
A village once known for its bandits became one of the most important addresses in Italian mural art.

Who it suits

A good stopover for anyone visiting the Supramonte or the Barbagia interior who's interested in political history and street art away from the coastal towns.
