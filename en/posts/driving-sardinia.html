Driving in Sardinia: Traffic, Roads & Rental Car Tips

Without your own car or a rental, getting around Sardinia is difficult – public transport outside the bigger cities is sparse. A few things worth knowing before your first drive.

Drive times are easily underestimated

Sardinia looks compact on a map – in reality, many coastal roads are winding and narrow, and villages slow traffic further. For 100 kilometres outside the SS131 expressway, plan on 1.5 to 2 hours rather than one.

The SS131 as the island's backbone

The "Carlo Felice" (SS131) is the only continuous expressway connecting Cagliari in the south with Sassari/Porto Torres in the north, and is usually the fastest link between the major cities. Away from it, many roads are single-lane in each direction, winding, and without a median.

Roadworks and temporary closures on coastal roads aren't unusual – staying flexible and keeping an alternate route in mind saves frustration.

Practical rental car notes

  • No toll roads: unlike mainland Italy, Sardinia has no toll motorways.
  • Prefer small cars: a compact rental pays off in narrow old-town lanes and on tight country roads.
  • Watch speed limits consistently: speed cameras are common, especially through towns.
  • Plan your fuel stops: petrol stations are sparser inland and away from the main towns, and many close at midday or on Sundays.
  • Wildlife crossings possible: especially at dusk, wild boars can cross roads suddenly (see our dedicated article).

Getting around without your own car

Intercity buses (ARST) run between the larger towns, serving coastal towns more frequently in peak season. But for flexible exploring of beaches and inland destinations, a rental car remains by far the more practical choice.

Practical tip: booking a rental car right on arrival at the airport (see our airports article) saves time and hassle – the major providers have counters right in the terminal at all three airports.
